What this filter doesn't see

  • QUIC (HTTP/3, UDP/443). Its ClientHello is encrypted. {{if .Data.QUICRejected}} Rejected from {{.Data.QUICRejected}} while the filter is on, so browsers fall back to TLS over TCP, where the name is seen. {{else}} Rejected from the filtered ports and their VLANs once the filter is on and applied. {{end}}
  • Encrypted Client Hello (ECH). The real name is encrypted; the filter sees only the outer, public one.
  • TLS on ports other than 443.
  • ClientHellos over several TLS records. They pass, counted as truncated. One split across TCP segments is followed: a segment out of order is dropped until TCP sends it in order, one with a bad checksum is dropped unread, and a hello longer than 16 segments, unfinished after 30 seconds or in a segment over 2,048 bytes is dropped.
  • Names of 32 bytes or more. Refused when you add them, so none is silently ineffective.
  • IPv6 with more than four extension headers, and TCP fragments. The filter reads IPv6 like IPv4, behind up to four extension headers. A packet with more, and a TCP fragment of either family, is dropped instead: no LAN device sends one, and the server would put a hello back together that the filter never saw whole.
